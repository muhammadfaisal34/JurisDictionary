How To Control Judges

Control the Judge with Rules & Law

1. Pleading

A lawsuit starts with complaints and answers.

The pleader must allege the essential facts that trigger the laws that compel the judge to rule in your favor.

If a complaint fails to allege every essential fact element required by law, the pleader has no cause of action, and the court has no power to enter judgment on that claim.

Key Rule of Pleading:

Allegations determine what law controls the judge. Only facts that trigger controlling statutes matter—keep irrelevant emotions out!

2. Motions to Dismiss

Attack defective pleadings before you file an answer!

If your opponent's complaint or counterclaim is missing even one essential fact element, file a timely Motion to Dismiss for Failure to State a Cause of Action.

When a complaint is legally insufficient, the rules of court and established precedent give the judge no discretion. The judge is legally required to dismiss the claim.

This is how you control the judge: by leaving the judge no legal choice but to dismiss your opponent's defective claim.

3. Discovery

Discovery is the engine of victory—where facts become proof on the record.

You have five powerful discovery tools to compel the truth under oath:

  • Requests for Admissions: Forces opponent to admit key facts under penalty of perjury. If they fail to answer in time, facts are automatically deemed admitted!
  • Requests for Production: Forces your opponent to produce documents, contracts, emails, and physical records they want to hide.
  • Interrogatories: Written questions answered under oath that lock down their sworn version of events.
  • Depositions & Subpoenas: In-person sworn testimony that traps opposing witnesses on the record before trial.

If your opponent hides records or refuses to answer, file a Motion to Compel and hold them in contempt of court until they obey.

4. Moving the Court

Judges cannot act until you "move" them.

A judge does not grant relief because you ask politely or complain. You must file formal written motions supported by points and authorities.

Every motion requires an accompanying Legal Memorandum explaining why the law mandates the requested order, and a Proposed Order drafted for the judge's signature.

The Rule of Motions:

Judges must either grant or deny your written motions on the record. They cannot ignore them. If they rule improperly, the written record guarantees a reversal on appeal.

5. Winning on Law

Judges are bound by law, not personal opinions or biases.

You do not win by persuading a judge with feelings. You win by citing mandatory controlling authority: statutes, constitutional mandates, and published appellate court decisions.

When you prove your essential facts on the record and cite the binding law that controls those facts, the judge has a mandatory duty to enter the order in your favor.

Controlling appellate law ties the judge's hands. The judge must obey the law or face public reversal by their appellate superiors.

6. Rules of Evidence

The Rules of Evidence are your fact filters!

You must know how to get your evidence IN to the court record, and how to keep your opponent's evidence OUT.

Use timely, precise objections to block improper testimony and inadmissible documents:

Hearsay

Out-of-court statements offered to prove the truth.

Foundation

Failing to prove authenticity before admission.

Relevance

Prejudicial info that does not prove essential facts.

Best Evidence

Original writings required over hearsay copies.

Everything STOPS until the judge rules on your objection. The judge must sustain or overrule on the record—this preserves error and protects your case on appeal!

7. Summary Judgment

Win your case completely on paper without ever going to trial!

A Motion for Summary Judgment asks the judge to enter a final judgment because all essential facts are proven and there is no genuine dispute for a jury to decide.

Support your motion with sworn affidavits, deposition transcripts, and admissions obtained during discovery. When no material facts remain in doubt, the law compels the judge to enter final judgment in your favor.

Most winnable cases should be won right here—on paper, before trial.

8. Conclusion & Core Strategy

Simplicity is your power. Controlling a judge is a 4-step formula:

1

ALLEGE the Essential Facts

Draft clear pleadings that trigger mandatory judge-controlling statutes.

2

PROVE the Facts on the Record

Use discovery to force sworn admissions and admitted documents into evidence.

3

MOVE the Court with Written Motions

File formal motions with legal memoranda and proposed orders for signature.

4

CONTROL the Judge with Binding Law

Cite controlling appellate precedent that leaves the judge zero legal discretion.

Just follow the steps JurisDictionary® teaches!
