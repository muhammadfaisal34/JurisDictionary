Winning Made Easy

Just the Tools You Need

Just Fit the Parts Together

Here are only a few parts to every case:

• Complaints (criminal and civil) that allege facts the pleader must prove to win.

• Answers that deny what complaints allege.

• Discovery both sides use to get evidence admitted to the court record to prove what's been alleged.

• Motions and Memoranda that present the laws that will decide the case based on the allegations and proof.

• Orders that compel the parties (and sometimes other people) to obey or be thrown in jail.

This simplicity is your power.

You don't have to be a rocket scientist to win.

Just follow the steps JurisDictionary® teaches.

Simplicity is Power

Your #1 Right is the right to know how to control judges and lawyers to get Justice!

If you're suing someone, allege and prove the essential facts that trigger laws that compel judges to rule in your favor.

If you're being sued, allege and prove the essential facts that show that your plaintiff has not alleged or cannot prove the essential fact of his complaint.

If you're accused of a crime, allege and prove that the prosecutor has not alleged or cannot prove the essential facts of the alleged crime beyond any doubt.

That's it!

What You Need

You need just 5 things to win:

• The essential facts of your case

• The laws that the facts trigger

• To get your evidence in the court record

• To keep your opponent's evidence out

• Motions and memoranda that compel the judge to enforce the law

I didn't learn any of this in law school.

I learned by fighting and winning.

The Law of the Case

The facts alleged trigger which law or laws control the judge. You must control the judge with law(s) and, of course, the rules.

Dog bite laws, for example, ask these questions:

• Did dog cause injury in the past?

• Was dog taunted by victim?

• Was victim on dog owner's property?

• Was dog under dog owner's control?

The law of your case will not be more complicated.

These facts are called essential facts.

They trigger the law(s) that control the judge.

No other facts matter, so keep other stuff out!

Rules of Procedure

The Rules of Procedure govern what the parties may do and when.

Everything must be done "in order" step-by-step.

One step at-a-time.

Don't let your opponent do anything "out of order".

The procedural rules are not rocket science.

Rules of Evidence

The rules of evidence are "fact filters".

They allow good facts in and keep bad facts out.

They are fair to both sides!

In my Evidence Class you will learn how to:

• Stop your opponent from offering evidence the rules don't allow

• Stop your opponent's witnesses from offering hearsay

• Stop your opponent from badgering your witnesses

• Stop your opponent from wasting the court's time with irrelevant facts

• Stop lay people from offering expert testimony

• Stop expert witnesses from offering lay testimony

• Stop unauthenticated documents from being offered as evidence

• Stop your opponent from misleading the judge or jury

• And much, much, much more!

It's easy once you see it this way.

Objections

Objections enforce the rules!

You will soon know how, when, and why to make objections.

They are critical to winning, because they control the judge!

They stop your opponent from cheating.

You will learn to do more than say "Objection!" like TV lawyers.

You'll say "Objection!" then state grounds for your objection that compel the judge to enforce the rules.

Everything STOPS until the judge rules on objections.

Sustained or denied.

Cannot be ignored.

Control the judge!

Moving the Court

Judges do nothing until you "move" them.

You do this with "motions" — written and spoken.

Every motion requires an order.

Move the court to order anything you want.

Judges must either grant or deny them.

They cannot ignore them.
